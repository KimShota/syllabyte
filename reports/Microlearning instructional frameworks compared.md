# Prediction and retrieval could outrank the CLT-bLM script

The frameworks most likely to beat the project's Prepare -> Initiate -> Deliver -> Deliver -> End script are not new skeletons. They are the same five segments with two learner actions added: a committed guess early (in Initiate) and a retrieve-then-reveal question at the end (replacing the recap). The top two candidates are a BOPPPS-style "bookended assessment" upgrade and a Predict-Observe-Explain (POE) hybrid. Condensed Gagne events, Merrill's First Principles, Component Display Theory, 4C/ID-style support fading, and Keller's ARCS follow, in that order. No study compares overall structures for videos under two minutes, so every ranking rests on evidence for the mechanisms underneath. The mechanism evidence is moderate: practice testing beats restudy by about g = 0.33 in classrooms (g = 0.50 overall), and prequestions with a forced guess reach g = 0.65 on the targeted content but about 0 on untargeted content. The framework-level evidence is weaker still, because Gagne and BOPPPS meta-analyses compare against plain lecture, and the Gagne RCT-only estimate (SMD 0.43) is far below its headline (1.55). CLT-bLM itself is the Cognitive Load Theory-based Lecture Model (Hadie et al.), built for one-hour health-professions lectures. It is not a microlearning model, and the project's End segment dropped the quiz and self-explanation elements the original model puts there. A realistic expectation for any upgrade is a small-to-medium delayed-retention gain (about g = 0.3 to 0.5 on practiced content, my inference), which only an A/B test with a delayed post-test can confirm. A test plan is at the end.

**Evidence flags used throughout.** [V] means the notes record that the figure was read in the paper's text or abstract. [S] means it came from a search summary, citing paper, blog or other secondary source, and should be re-checked before it is quoted. Where the notes gave no flag I defaulted to [S]. [BK] means background knowledge the researchers could not source. I use [BK] only for framework definitions and steps, never for effect sizes.

## CLT-bLM is a one-hour lecture model, and the project dropped its retrieval element

CLT-bLM stands for **Cognitive Load Theory-based Lecture Model** (Hadie and colleagues, Universiti Malaysia Sarawak and Universiti Sains Malaysia), a guideline for ordinary roughly one-hour face-to-face or online lectures in health-professions education. The "b" means "based", not "bite-sized". It has **four phases (Preparation, Initiation, Delivery, End) and 27 strategies**, built from cognitive load theory and the cognitive theory of multimedia learning ([Frontiers in Medicine 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)) [V, fetch summary; the last 35,000 characters of the paper were not read]. In the original, Preparation is the lecturer's design work before class (taxonomy-based outcomes, chunking, plain slides), not a learner-facing hook. Initiation holds a short pre-lecture activity, prior-knowledge activation and the stated purpose. Delivery holds intra-lecture activities, a break after 10-15 minutes, dual-mode presentation, examples and analogies. **End holds summaries, short quizzes, self-explanation questions and post-class consultation** ([PMC8520998](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8520998/)) [V for the phase table]. The project's "Deliver x2" is not in the source model, and the notes found no source that applies CLT-bLM to videos shorter than a lecture or to CS. The notes also ruled out the obvious alternative attribution. Khong and Kabilan's micro-learning model is a CLT + multimedia + self-determination model for second-language learning with no Prepare/Initiate/Deliver/End phases ([UniKL repository](https://ir.unikl.edu.my/jspui/handle/123456789/25271)) [S], and a CLT-microlearning higher-education study contains no mention of CLT-bLM ([ERIC EJ1423546](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1423546.pdf)) [V, full text extracted].

The evidence for CLT-bLM is thin and confounded. The 2021 study (n = 215 medicine, dentistry and nutrition students) was a pre-post comparison with no control group. A conventional face-to-face lecture before lockdown was compared with a CLT-based online lecture about two months later, so topic, mode, timing and lecturer preparation all differed ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)) [V]. Comprehension gain was 34.7 versus 54.2 (p < 0.001) on a five-item test. Intrinsic load fell by about 2.4 points and extraneous load by about 1.1 (the two notes round the intrinsic figure slightly differently, 2.4 versus 2.5, which is not a real conflict). **No effect sizes were reported and retention was not measured.** The motivation result is internally inconsistent: the text says it improved, but the table shows 5.8 falling to 5.6 [V]. Two earlier randomised trials by the same group exist. The first (*Innovations in Education and Teaching International*) reportedly improved knowledge acquisition and retention ([UNIMAS repository](https://ir.unimas.my/id/eprint/14472/)) [S, abstract-level], and its year is listed as 2016 in one record and 2018 (55(4)) in another. The second (*Journal of Taibah University Medical Sciences*, 2018, 197 participants) found higher engagement, but the control lecture came first and the CLT-bLM lecture three weeks later ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/2b8c146510154edc95b2f2022b693e2e)) [S]. A search summary mentioned a 99-student trial and "effect sizes 0.404 to 1.696"; neither figure traces to a primary page, so **treat both as unverified**.

The mismatch with the project matters for the ranking. The project's script treats Prepare as a hook or prior-knowledge cue, Initiate as the objective, and End as a recap. The original End includes a **quiz and a self-explanation prompt**, so the project's End, which only re-states the idea, **dropped the retrieval element** the original contains. A re-statement is closest to the restudy control in the testing-effect literature, which is why restoring retrieval is the highest-leverage change available. The cognitive-load production rules underneath CLT-bLM (signaling, coherence, segmenting, modality) have their own independent evidence and should stay as constraints on any new structure.

## No study compares structures, so the ranking rests on mechanisms of modest and unequal strength

The notes searched for any head-to-head comparison of event structures in short video (problem-first versus concept-first, recap versus retrieval, hook versus no hook) and found none. The closest direct evidence is microlearning meta-analyses, but those do not isolate structure, length, spacing or testing. A five-study higher-education synthesis found a pooled mean difference of 12.6 marks (95% CI 1.2 to 23.9) against any session longer than 15 minutes ([Senadheera et al. 2024](https://journals.kln.ac.lk/jmtr/media/attachments/2024/08/28/jmtr_24_22.pdf)) [S]. Two larger SMDs (0.74 for higher education, 0.90 for undergraduate medicine) come from ResearchGate records whose peer-review status and authorship the notes could not verify, so **I do not rely on them**. The best-controlled spacing meta-analysis gives only SMD 0.32 for knowledge ([JMIR 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11502984/)) [S], and video that replaces existing teaching gives g = 0.28 versus 0.80 when added to it ([Noetel et al. 2021](https://www.aera.net/tabid/19324/mid/50329/default.aspx)) [S]. The notes also found no RCT of short-video microlearning for university CS or STEM students, and a smart-learning-environments meta-analysis (a different intervention class) found engineering and technology effects smaller than humanities, with the engineering effect of 0.127 not significant ([Frontiers in Education 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1829962/full)) [V, fetch summary]. Treat that as a caution on expected size, not a finding about this design.

The table lists the mechanism-level figures that drive the ranking. Figures from Mayer-lab medians (d about 0.5 to 1.7) are consistently larger than independent meta-analyses (g about 0.2 to 0.5), so I use the independent figures ([Mayer 2017 listing](https://www.metatoc.com/papers/105408-using-multimedia-for-e-learning)) [S].

Two conclusions follow. First, retrieval and guessed-prediction have the cleanest quantitative support, and both fit in a few seconds of pause. Second, two features the project might add are not supported in video. Embedded self-explanation prompts and navigation features produced no gain in a controlled experiment (N = 128), and one study found prompts raised cognitive load without benefit ([Beege and Ploetzner 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-024-09693-5)) [S]. In Barbieri et al., adding self-explanation prompts to worked examples moderated the effect negatively [S], which contradicts the general Bisra result. CLT-bLM's own End phase recommends a self-explanation prompt, so the retrieval question should not be a self-explanation prompt. Embedded questions in video overall are mixed: Szpunar et al. found interpolated tests helped in a 21-minute lecture, but Jing et al. did not replicate that in a 40-minute lecture ([PMC3631699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699/)) [S]. Learners also skip interactions: after edX videos, the share who attempted the next problem fell from 56% to 31% as videos lengthened ([Guo, Kim and Rubin 2014](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)) [V]. Retrieval in maths is also inconclusive (7 studies, 32 effects; [Murray et al. 2025](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/229807)) [S], so for CS the questions should be application-level (trace, predict), not definition recall.

## Ranked frameworks, with evidence and a mapping onto the five segments

Rankings combine mechanism evidence, fit to 60-90 s (about 150-225 spoken words, with at most two 3-5 s pauses, or about 20-25% of runtime), and how little change the existing pipeline needs. The ranking is my judgment, not a finding from any source. All mappings keep the segment order prepare, initiate, deliver, deliver, end.

| Rank | Framework | Prepare | Initiate | Deliver 1 | Deliver 2 | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BOPPPS (bookended assessment) | Bridge-in: a content-relevant question or scenario, objective as one clause | Pre-assessment: one forced-guess question, 3-4 s pause, no reveal yet | Participatory: core idea, signaled | Participatory: level-specific example (below) | Post-assessment: a new question, pause, reveal, then a one-line summary |
| 2 | POE hybrid (Predict-Observe-Explain, with Merrill's problem-first framing) | A concrete CS artifact (code, trace, data structure) | Predict: commit to an outcome, 3-4 s | Observe and explain: animation runs, concept resolves the discrepancy | Elaborate: L1 full example, L2 key steps, L3 change one parameter ("what if") | Retrieve: one application question, then answer |
| 3 | Gagne events, condensed | Gain attention, objective as a clause | Recall prior learning | Present content | Guidance via worked example | Elicit performance, feedback, retention and transfer cue |
| 4 | Merrill First Principles | Problem | Activation | Demonstration | Application | Integration |

### 1. BOPPPS-style bookended assessment: the lowest-risk upgrade

BOPPPS (Bridge-in, Objectives, Pre-assessment, Participatory learning, Post-assessment, Summary) is the closest framework to CLT-bLM's skeleton and adds exactly the two missing learner actions ([Li 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2024.1319711/full)). Its framework-level evidence is two meta-analyses against lecture: 41 studies (34 RCTs, 5,042 medical students) found better knowledge, skill and comprehensive scores ([Ma 2022](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.975229/text)), and 13 RCTs (2,991 nursing students) found higher theory, practice and self-learning scores, graded low to very low certainty for some outcomes and moderate for self-learning ([Li 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2024.1319711/full)) [S]. The notes did not retrieve pooled effect sizes. These comparisons are against lecture and conflate structure with added activity, so they support the pieces rather than the sequence. The active ingredients are the strongest in the table: prequestion with guess (g = 0.65 [V]) and retrieval over restudy (g = 0.33 [S] to 0.50 [V]). The expected gain over the current script is the retrieval-versus-recap delta, about g = 0.3 to 0.5 for practiced content and about 0.3 for untested content (inference). Because the End prompt and the delayed test are both retrieval, make the End prompt retrieve the idea in a new example and keep the delayed transfer item different from both.

By level, the notes' inference is that L1 gets a factual, recognition-level prequestion (factual prequestions outperformed conceptual ones, 0.58 vs 0.28 [V]) and a multiple-choice End check on the worked example. L2 gets "identify the next step or the error". L3 gets a conceptual prediction in Initiate. Deliver 2 stays as in the pipeline. This also fits CLT-bLM's own authors' principles, since the original already includes prior-knowledge activation and quizzes.

### 2. POE hybrid: higher ceiling, higher risk, and the best match to animated CS

POE (White and Gunstone, per [BK]) and the 5E learning cycle are science-teaching cycles that put a prediction before the observation. For animation, the "observation" is running the code or tracing the algorithm. The mechanism evidence is the guess moderator (0.65 vs 0.22) and the prequestion effects above [V]. The framework-level evidence is unreliable: learning-cycle meta-analyses report Hedges' g = 1.121 over 123 studies and 1.845 for 5E in acid-base chemistry, mostly from small, lower-impact studies likely inflated by publication bias, and no standalone POE meta-analysis was found ([Turkish learning-cycle meta-analyses](https://openaccess.artvin.edu.tr/items/92f0e7cc-da9a-4f2e-860d-57801b98b1d2); [5E physics](https://journal.uinjkt.ac.id/index.php/edusains/article/view/36339)) [S]. Do not cite those numbers as expected gains. POE ranks second only because it compresses the core idea (the notes' candidate allocates 12-15 s to observing and 15-20 s to explaining), so a weak prediction step costs real content time, and 3-5 s pauses may be skipped.

POE gives the cleanest handling of the project's levels. L3's "what if" Deliver 2 is literally POE's Elaborate step. L3's End can reveal the answer to that "what if", which keeps duration and doubles as a transfer-level retrieval. L1 keeps its full worked example in Deliver 2 and uses a low-stakes prediction on a familiar case. L2's single worked example with key steps can leave one step blank before revealing it. Problem-first has a conditional evidence base. Productive-failure meta-analysis figures conflict (g = 0.87 bias-corrected in one mirror versus 0.55 and 0.64 in a working paper by the same authors; 53 studies and 166 comparisons versus 33 articles and 118 comparisons in an ETH record) ([ETH record](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/93a6fe24-dde9-4f59-ad42-fc4476064bdf)) [S]. Procedural content shows no special benefit ([Mazziotti et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6435728/)) [S], and a Bayesian-reasoning replication found no main effect [S]. In a 75-second video, problem-first reduces to a 3-4 s guess, so the productive-failure literature is only indirect support. Use prediction for conceptual and principle units at L2-L3, and explanation-first for procedural units at L1.

### 3. Condensed Gagne: useful as a menu, not a sequence

A 2025 meta-analysis of 11 studies (825 participants, health professions, versus lecture) reports knowledge SMD 1.55 (95% CI 0.81-2.29, I2 = 95%), practice SMD 1.83, compliance OR 4.92 and satisfaction OR 7.86. By design, **RCTs give SMD 0.43 (0.17-0.68, I2 = 0%) and cohort studies 2.20 (1.08-3.32, I2 = 97%)**, so the credible estimate is about 0.4 ([Li et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1522830/full)) [S; the notes give no read-in-full flag]. The study mix is reported as 4 RCTs and 7 cohorts in the Results text but 5 and 6 in the abstract. The meta-analysis does not report which events each study used, so it does not show the nine-event order matters. Nine events in 75 seconds would leave about 8 seconds each (the nine events are [BK]), so use events 1, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 9: gain attention, recall prior learning, present, elicit performance, feedback, and a retention and transfer cue. That is the same upgrade as BOPPPS (elicit performance and feedback replace the recap) with a different label, so it adds little beyond rank 1.

### 4. Merrill's First Principles: right instincts, correlational evidence

Merrill's principles (problem-centered, activation, demonstration, application, integration) map cleanly onto five beats, but the evidence is the weakest of the top four. Frick et al. (2010, n = 464) found students who perceived the principles were about four times more likely to reach high mastery, with correlational, perception-based data that the authors themselves say is under-tested ([Frick](https://tedfrick.sitehost.iu.edu/AECTresearchAwardFrick.pdf)) [S]. A MOOC experiment (Badali et al. 2022, n = 335) favoured a Merrill-based design on learning and satisfaction, with no effect size seen ([Agris record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/669f714a00eb85b7d72d0046)) [S]. No meta-analysis was found, and a 2019 *ETR&D* paper is cited as arguing the principles are not universal ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11423-019-09660-9)) [S, unread]. Its main value for this project is the problem-first framing of Prepare, which POE already uses. For the second Deliver, Application is what the project's levels already implement (L1 shown, L2 partly shown, L3 learner tries the "what if").

### 5-8. Layers and level logic: Component Display Theory, 4C/ID fading, ARCS, productive failure

**Component Display Theory** (Merrill 1983 [BK]) is a script-template selector. Concept units get a definition, an example, a non-example and practice; procedure units get steps, a worked example and practice; principle units get a rule, a case and a prediction. The notes found no evidence for it (treat as weak, [BK]). Its value is that the performance level (remember, use, find) can be set per learner level, and it pairs with Bloom-aligned verbs so that the objective, the practice prompt and the End check share one verb (no controlled evidence for alignment per se). **4C/ID** (van Merrienboer) has a pooled d = 0.79 on performance with higher education a significant moderator ([Costa et al. 2022](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/publications/the-four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-a-meta-analysis-on-use-and-effect/81383?lang=en)) [S, abstract-level], but it is a course-level redesign effect, not a single-video effect. Its usable piece is the completion strategy (worked example, then completion problem, then full problem). That would change L2 from "one worked example with key steps" to a worked example with one blank step, matching the notes' adaptive-fading inference. The expertise-reversal figures in the table are the support for the direction of the level logic [S]. Barbieri et al. cut the other way, finding no reliable prior-knowledge moderation for worked examples against problem solving [S]. So the case for L3's "what if" rests on guidance overload for experts, not on "worked examples hurt experts". **ARCS** (Keller [BK]) has a meta-analysis of 38 controlled experiments (110 effect sizes, about 8,690 students) with achievement ES = 0.74 and motivation ES = 0.43 ([Göksu and Bolat 2021](https://gcris.artuklu.edu.tr/entities/publication/bcdc76ff-9439-4f73-afb2-9934efe40eb6)) [S], but it is an overlay, not a sequence, and the studies are full lessons. Fold Attention and Relevance into Prepare as a content-relevant question, since seductive details cost about g = -0.33 (-0.16 in a multilevel analysis; [Sundararajan and Adesope 2020](https://www.e-teaching.org/materialien/literatur/sundararajan-adesope-2020)) [S]. **Productive failure** is the weakest fit (see rank 2), so use it only as an L3 option.

### Not recommended as replacements

Elaboration Theory, Dick and Carey, and Context-Content-Practice have no evidence located in the notes, and the last has no identifiable canonical source (treat as weak or unverified). The same holds for taxonomic microlearning frameworks (Hug, Jomah), which describe what microlearning is and are not instructional procedures with evidence. The reviews converge on the same de facto structure the project already has (one objective, chunking, multimedia design, interactivity, feedback), but that is consensus from low-rigor studies, not causal evidence ([Monib et al.](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11774797/fullTextXML)) [V, first 100,000 characters]. "Hook-Teach-Check" and the "Hook Model" are practitioner heuristics with no validation, and the Hook Model is a consumer-product framework, not a learning one.

## Conflicts and unverified figures to resolve before citing

Several figures conflict across the notes, and none should be quoted without checking the primary paper. The Gagne headline (1.55) and its RCT-only estimate (0.43) differ by a factor of about 3.6, and the RCT subgroup is the credible one. Productive-failure effect sizes and study counts conflict across mirrors, as above. The practice-testing meta-analysis (Adesope) is reported with 118 or 188 articles, g = 0.61 or 0.70, and no feedback moderation, while Rowland finds feedback matters (0.73 vs 0.39, via Yang) and Yang finds it matters (0.537 vs 0.374), so the feedback evidence is inconsistent; reveal the answer in the video regardless, since it costs 7-10 seconds and no source shows it hurts. Case comparison is d = 0.50 or 0.60. Signaling study counts are 103 or 145. Self-explanation prompts have g = 0.55 in general but a negative moderating or null role with worked examples and video. The seductive-details effect is -0.33 versus -0.16. Expertise reversal and the Mayer-corpus claim that university and STEM learners benefit most ([Hendrick summary](https://carlhendrick.substack.com/p/which-multimedia-strategies-actually)) [S, authors and title not verified] sit uneasily with the smaller engineering effect above, though the latter concerns a different intervention class. The prequestion citation King-Shepard et al. (2025; 0.66 vs 0.01) could not be traced to the paper and is unverified [S]. The Hadie "0.404 to 1.696" and "99 students" figures, the two ResearchGate microlearning meta-analyses, and the interpolated-retrieval figures (g = 0.69 online, 0.76 lab; [Chan, Ahn and Szpunar 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11965562), only three online studies) [S] are likewise unverified.

Evidence on short-form video itself is thin and points both ways. A 57-participant fMRI study found lower recall accuracy for fragmented short clips than one continuous video, using travel clips rather than lessons ([npj Science of Learning 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-025-00399-y)) [S, text not accessed]. A 62-student comparison found LLM-generated reels scored 93.85% versus 79.72% for a long lecture, but only on an immediate test in a custom interface ([arXiv 2509.05962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.05962)) [S]. The practical reading is that a self-contained narrative unit with a closing consolidation beat (retrieve, then reveal) is safer than a fragmented one, and that immediate-quiz wins prove little. Segmenting into 3-5 bursts of about 10-20 s with 1-2 s of silence follows from the transient-information literature, but the burst length is a design heuristic, not an evidenced optimum ([Leahy and Sweller 2011](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/cognitive-load-theory-and-the-effects-of-transient-information-on/)) [S].

## A/B test plan: a 2x2 plus a POE arm, a seven-day delayed test, and level-split analysis

The design isolates the two proposed additions rather than comparing bundled structures, because no source says which element of a bundle does the work.

| Arm | Initiate | End | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| C0 control | Objective statement (current) | Recap (current) | Baseline |
| C1 prequestion only | Forced-guess question, 3-4 s pause | Recap | Value of the early guess |
| C2 retrieval only | Objective statement | New question, pause, reveal, one-line summary | Value of replacing the recap |
| C3 BOPPPS-style | Forced-guess question | Retrieve-then-reveal | Combined upgrade (rank 1) |
| C4 POE hybrid | Concrete artifact, predict, observe, explain | Retrieve-then-reveal; at L3 the End reveals the "what if" answer | Rank 2 against rank 1 |

Hold constant across arms: total duration (60-90 s, within about 5 s), word count, the signaling, coherence, segmenting and neural-voice production rules, the topic, and the visual assets. Pauses must be present or absent, not just shorter, and each arm needs the same player controls.

The primary outcome is a **delayed post-test at 7 days**. Testing effects are larger at 1+ day delays (g = 0.69 vs 0.41 same-day, Rowland via a blog [S]) and Adesope found the best effects at 1-6 days [S]. A same-session quiz therefore understates the benefit and is a poor measure of delayed retention. Add an optional 28-day test if the course timetable allows. Each delayed test needs three kinds of item: a retention item in the same format as the video (so format matching does not favor any arm; Adesope found matching increases effects [S]), a **transfer item** in a new context for the concept (quizzing transfers less than it retains, 0.32 vs 0.50 [V]), and an item on a concept the video did not prequestion, to test the general-versus-targeted prequestion gap (0.54 vs 0.04 [V]). Make sure the End question and the delayed items are not identical for any arm. Secondary measures are a one-item cognitive-load rating, a logged response or tap at each pause (to measure skipping, since pauses may be ignored and the notes found no evidence on whether a covert pause gives the same retrieval benefit), completion, watch time, and viewer confidence.

Split by level: analyze L1, L2 and L3 as separate strata and report results by level, with level as a pre-registered moderator. Hypotheses, which are inferences from the mechanisms and not tested results: L1 benefits from C1 and C2 (factual prequestion, multiple-choice End), and C4 is no better than C3 and may be worse because of load; L3 benefits most from C4 because its "what if" Deliver 2 and conceptual prediction match the guidance-reduction logic. A useful add-on tests the level logic itself: give a random subset of L3 learners the L1 Deliver 2 (full worked example) and vice versa, which tests expertise reversal directly on this pipeline. For analysis, fit a mixed-effects model with random intercepts for learner and concept, with arm, level and arm-by-level as fixed effects, and report Hedges' g with confidence intervals. For power, detecting g = 0.3 with 80% power at two-sided alpha = .05 needs about 175 learners per arm per level in a between-subjects design (my arithmetic, ignoring clustering). That is large for a capstone, so use a within-learner design: each learner sees different concepts under different arms in a counterbalanced Latin-square order, which makes concepts the limiting sample and needs enough concepts per arm to estimate the concept variance. Pilot to estimate it before fixing the sample. A reasonable pre-specified adoption rule is a delayed-test gain of g of at least 0.2 with a confidence interval excluding zero, in at least the levels where the hypotheses predict it (this threshold is a suggestion, not from a source).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. CLT-bLM is not a rival structure to beat but a lecture checklist whose useful content (load-management rules) belongs in the production layer, while the narrative skeleton can be swapped for something that makes learners do something. The upgrade with the best evidence per second of runtime is also the cheapest: ask for a guess early, replace the recap with retrieve-then-reveal, and keep the project's level-specific Deliver 2. Everything else in the ranking either contributes the same mechanism under a different label (Gagne, Merrill) or is a layer (CDT, 4C/ID fading, ARCS).

Two things would change this ranking. If the A/B test shows the guess and the retrieval pause are skipped or add load in 60-90 s, the effects measured in lectures and labs do not transfer to this format, and the fallback is the unchanged script with stronger production rules. If L3 learners do better with the full worked example than with the "what if", the project's level logic needs revisiting even though the expertise-reversal figures (about +0.5 and -0.43, [S]) predict otherwise.
