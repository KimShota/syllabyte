# Instructional design frameworks and "event" models that could replace or improve the CLT-bLM script structure for 60-90 s micro-videos

Conventions used in these notes
- Every claim with a URL was seen in a search result or fetched page during this research session. Several are snippets of secondary summaries; those are flagged where the primary text was not read.
- Items tagged **[BK]** are background knowledge (well-known textbook facts about each framework's origin and steps). I did not verify them against a source this session, so the report writer should treat them as unverified unless re-checked.
- Evidence tiers used: STRONG = meta-analysis or multiple controlled experiments directly testing the framework or its core mechanism; MODERATE = meta-analysis with weak primary studies or heavy heterogeneity; WEAK = widely taught, mostly correlational, perception-based or single-study.

## 1. What is CLT-bLM, where does it come from, and how should it be critiqued?

### Takeaway
"CLT-bLM" is almost certainly the **Cognitive Load Theory-based Lecture Model** (Hadie et al.), where "b" means "based" (not "bite-sized" or "micro"). It was designed for roughly one-hour university lectures and bundles 27 load-management strategies into four lecture phases. It is not a microlearning model, and the 60-90 s "Prepare/Initiate/Deliver/End" script is an adaptation of it rather than the model itself. Evidence for the model is limited to a few medical/health-professions education studies, with the best retention evidence (a 2016 RCT) not retrievable here.

### Cited Findings
- A search for "CLT-bLM" returns the paper "Cognitive Load Theory-based Lecture Model (CLT-bLM): An Evidence-based Strategy For Effective Lecturing" on ResearchGate. The expansion is Cognitive Load Theory-based Lecture Model. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309633360_Cognitive_Load_Theory-based_Lecture_Model_CLT-bLM_An_Evidence-based_Strategy_For_Effective_Lecturing) (page itself returned 403; title seen in search result only)
- The model is attributed to Hadie et al. as "a lecturing guideline"; the core citation is Hadie, S.N.H., Hassan, A., Ismail, Z.I.M., Ismail, H.N., Talip, S.B., Rahim, A.F.A. (2016/2018), "Empowering students' minds through a cognitive load theory-based lecture model: a metacognitive approach", *Innovations in Education and Teaching International* 55(4):398-407. The online version/repository lists 2016 and the print volume 2018. The abstract describes a randomised controlled trial comparing groups on cognitive load, self-perceived learning, knowledge acquisition and knowledge retention. — [UNIMAS repository](https://ir.unimas.my/id/eprint/14472/); [Frontiers 2021 citing it](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)
- The model has **four phases underlying 27 lecturing strategies**, and is built from CLT effects (modality, split-attention, isolated-interacting elements, redundancy, goal-free, guidance fading, worked example, completion example) plus CTML principles (signaling, contiguity, segmenting, coherence, pre-training, personalization, voice). — [Frontiers in Medicine 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)
- Phase content as described in that paper (only strategies used in that study are listed, not all 27):
  - **Preparation** (lecturer-side design work before the lecture): taxonomy-based learning outcomes, information chunking, plain backgrounds, headings, visual cues, colour coding, trimming diagrams and labels. Principles: chunking, coherence, signaling.
  - **Initiation**: short pre-lecture activity (video or quiz), stating the purpose, outcomes and outline, anticipating learning difficulties. Principles: prior-knowledge activation, attention focus, pre-training.
  - **Delivery**: intra-lecture activities (audio-response quizzes), revisiting prior knowledge, dual-mode presentation, examples and analogies, a break after 10-15 min, pace and intonation. Principles: modality, redundancy, split-attention, segmenting, contiguity, voice.
  - **End**: summarising, **self-explanation prompts, short quizzes** and references, disclosing expectations, post-class consultation. Principles: germane load, self-explanation, self-assessment.
  — [Frontiers in Medicine 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)
- Evidence, 2021 online-lecture study (Frontiers in Medicine, n = 215 medical/dental/nutrition students, USM Malaysia): pre-post, within-subject, **fixed order and confounded** (face-to-face conventional lecture before lockdown versus CLT-based online lecture about two months after, with 8 weeks of lecturer preparation). Comprehension gain 34.7 (face-to-face) versus 54.2 (CLT online), p < 0.001. Intrinsic load fell 6.3 to 3.8, extraneous load 2.0 to 0.9. **No effect sizes were reported, retention was not measured**, and the motivation results text and table contradict each other (table shows a decrease). Authors acknowledge the non-counterbalanced design and limited generalisability beyond medical/allied health. — [Frontiers in Medicine 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)
- A second Hadie study (J. Taibah Univ. Med. Sci., 2018; RCT, 197 participants, three institutions) found the guideline stimulated cognitive engagement and learning experience; it focused on engagement and motivation, not retention. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/2b8c146510154edc95b2f2022b693e2e)
- Secondary summaries say CLT-based lecturing produced better knowledge acquisition and retention than "freestyle lecturing" in the 2016 RCT, but I could not retrieve the numbers or effect sizes. — [UNIMAS repository](https://ir.unimas.my/id/eprint/14472/) (abstract-level only)
- Separately, a literature search found a 2025 Heliyon microlearning framework built on Andragogy in Practice, Bloom and CLT (three components: individual, situational, subject differences), and an adaptive microlearning study in *Scientific Reports* (2024). Neither uses Prepare/Initiate/Deliver/End labels. — [Heliyon via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2b4da6d99a2a49608c3fde49b9b3174d); [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-77122-1)

### Inferences
- The premise that bLM = "bite-sized Learning Model" is wrong; the team's own documentation should say "CLT-based Lecture Model". Citing it as a microlearning model invites reviewer pushback.
- Mapping to the project's script is loose. In the original, "Prepare" is the lecturer's pre-class design work (slide hygiene, chunking), not a learner-facing hook; "Initiate" combines pre-activity, prior-knowledge activation, purpose and outcomes; "End" includes self-explanation and quizzing, not just recap. The project's "Prepare = hook/prior knowledge, Initiate = objective, End = recap" therefore **drops the retrieval/self-explanation element that the original model includes in End**, and moves design hygiene (coherence, signaling, segmenting) out of the script into the production layer (where it still belongs).
- CLT-bLM is a checklist of load-management strategies placed on a lecture timeline, not an event model that specifies learner activities in a causal sequence. Its learning-outcome evidence is mostly one research group, medical/health students, 1-hour lectures, and confounded or perception-heavy outcomes. It should be regarded as WEAK-to-MODERATE support for the specific structure.
- The cognitive-load principles underneath (segmenting, signaling, coherence, modality, worked examples) have independent, stronger evidence (CLT/CTML literature [BK]). The best way to keep CLT-bLM's value is to retain those as production rules and swap the **narrative skeleton** for something retrieval- or problem-based.

### Gaps
- Could not read Hadie et al. 2016/2018 full text (paywalled; ResearchGate 403), so I do not have the retention effect size or the full list of 27 strategies.
- Could not confirm who coined the Prepare/Initiate/Deliver/End labels in the project's script beyond the paper's Preparation/Initiation/Delivery/End labels.

## 2. Framework-by-framework profiles (origin, steps, evidence, limits, fit, script mapping)

Time budget assumption for mappings: a 60-90 s narrated video is roughly 150-225 spoken words, so each "segment" is ~10-25 s. Use 5-7 beats maximum.

### 2.1 Gagne's Nine Events of Instruction
- **Origin [BK]:** Robert Gagne, *The Conditions of Learning* (1965; revised 1985), derived from an information-processing view of learning.
- **Steps [BK]:** (1) gain attention, (2) inform learner of objectives, (3) stimulate recall of prior learning, (4) present stimulus material, (5) provide learning guidance, (6) elicit performance, (7) provide feedback, (8) assess performance, (9) enhance retention and transfer.
- **Evidence (MODERATE, weak primary studies):** Li et al. (2025), *Frontiers in Medicine*, systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 studies (4 RCTs + 7 cohort per Results text; abstract says 5 + 6), 825 participants, health-professions education, vs lecture-based learning. Knowledge exam SMD 1.55 (95% CI 0.81-2.29, I² = 95%); practice score SMD 1.83 (1.19-2.47, I² = 90%); learning compliance OR 4.92; satisfaction OR 7.86. **Subgroup by design: RCTs SMD 0.43 (0.17-0.68, I² = 0%) vs cohort studies SMD 2.20 (1.08-3.32, I² = 97%)**, so the headline is heavily inflated by weak designs; the RCT-only estimate (~0.4) is the credible one. The paper does not report which events each study implemented, and quality was generally low with small samples. — [Li et al. 2025, Frontiers in Medicine](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1522830/full)
- Other evidence found is single-study: a Pakistani quasi-experiment measured lecture approval, not learning; a Philippine PE lesson-planning study measured perceived usefulness. — [PAFMJ](https://www.pafmj.org/PAFMJ/article/view/1009); [MSEUF](https://mseuf.edu.ph/research/read/1075)
- **Limitations:** Comparator is plain lecture (a weak control), so the effect cannot be attributed to the event ordering versus simply adding active elements and feedback. No component-level evidence about which events matter. Many events (practice, feedback, assessment) require learner interaction.
- **Fit for 60-90 s video:** Poor as a complete sequence (nine events in 75 s means each ~8 s), but good as a **menu**: events 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9 are the useful ones.
- **Mapping (condensed Gagne):** Gain attention (5-8 s) → recall prior knowledge (8 s, merge with hook) → present content (20 s) → guidance via worked example (20 s) → elicit performance via a pause-and-answer prompt (8 s) → feedback (reveal answer, 7 s) → transfer/retention cue (5-8 s). Objectives (event 2) can be one clause inside the hook.

### 2.2 Merrill's First Principles of Instruction and Pebble-in-the-Pond
- **Origin [BK]:** Merrill (2002), "First principles of instruction", *ETR&D* 50(3), 43-59; synthesis of instructional design theories. Pebble-in-the-Pond (Merrill 2002, *Performance Improvement* 41(7)) is the development process that starts from a whole task and expands outward.
- **Steps:** Problem-/task-centered, activation of prior knowledge, demonstration, application, integration (the five phases are the same whether described as principles or as a cycle). Merrill's claim is that learning is promoted when these are present. — [Learning Guild summary](https://www.learningguild.com/articles/research-for-practitioners-are-there-basic-principles-across-all-instructional-design-models); [Cortland ID theories](https://web.cortland.edu/frieda/ID/IDtheories/44.html)
- **Evidence (WEAK-to-MODERATE):**
  - Frick, Chadha, Watson & Zlatkovska (2010), *ETR&D* 58(2), 115-136 (n = 464 students, 12 courses; TALQ questionnaire): students who perceived first principles and academic learning time were roughly 4 times more likely to reach high mastery as rated by independent instructors. Correlational and perception-based; the authors themselves say few empirical studies verify the claim. — [Frick AECT award doc](https://tedfrick.sitehost.iu.edu/AECTresearchAwardFrick.pdf); [Frick 2010 listing](https://bio.tedfrick.me/etrd/FrickTALQ2009.pdf)
  - Badali et al. (2022), MOOC experiment with pre-post control group, n = 335: Merrill-based design favoured on learning and satisfaction; effect sizes not seen in snippet. — [Agris record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/669f714a00eb85b7d72d0046)
  - A 2019 *ETR&D* paper (Springer, s11423-019-09660-9) is cited as concluding that the first principles are not universal and suggesting an additional affective-domain principle. I did not read it. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11423-019-09660-9)
  - **No meta-analysis with pooled effect sizes for Merrill's principles was found.**
- **Limitations:** Principles are descriptive, not a fixed sequence, and are broad enough to be hard to falsify. Designed for task-centered courses, not 75-s explainers.
- **Fit:** Good conceptually (problem-first, application, integration), fair practically; the five phases map neatly to five script beats.
- **Mapping:** Problem (10 s: a realistic task or puzzle) → activation (8 s: link to what they know, ideally as a question) → demonstration (25 s: show how the concept solves the problem, with signalled steps) → application (15 s: pause prompt "try the next step" then reveal) → integration (10 s: one-sentence transfer to a new context or "explain it to someone"). Pebble-in-the-Pond is a curriculum design process; at the single-video level it reduces to "start with the whole task".

### 2.3 Component Display Theory (CDT)
- **Origin [BK]:** Merrill (1983), classifies content (fact, concept, procedure, principle) and performance level (remember, use, find) and prescribes presentation forms: generality (rule), example, practice (expository vs inquisitory), with helps (prompts, mnemonics, feedback).
- **Evidence:** Not located in this session. Historically tested by Merrill's group in small experiments; I did not retrieve any meta-analysis. Treat as WEAK (widely taught as a taxonomy). **[BK]**
- **Limitations:** Content-type classification is subjective; assumes expository or inquisitory sequence per micro-content type.
- **Fit:** Very good as a **script-template selector** for "knowledge units" in a CS course: concept units → definition + examples + non-example + classification practice; procedure units → steps + worked example + practice; principle units → rule + case + prediction. Maps well to leveled instruction because "performance level" (remember/use/find) can be varied by learner level.
- **Mapping:** Generality (definition/rule, 15 s) → example (20 s) → non-example or contrast (10 s) → practice item (pause + answer, 15 s) → helps/feedback (10 s). Level 1: more examples, lower performance level (use). Level 3: fewer examples, "find"-level (generalise to a new case).

### 2.4 Keller's ARCS motivational model
- **Origin [BK]:** John Keller (1979/1983/1987); Attention, Relevance, Confidence, Satisfaction. One source says 1979; most cite 1987. — [Göksu & Bolat 2021 record](https://acikerisim.dicle.edu.tr/items/cb19a896-421d-4c54-a441-92304f664a55/full)
- **Evidence (MODERATE):** Göksu & Islam Bolat (2021), *Review of Education* 9(1), 27-52: meta-analysis of 38 controlled experiments, 110 effect sizes, ~8,690 students (K-12 and higher ed). Achievement ES = 0.74 (medium), motivation ES = 0.43 (small); attention component had a very large ES; achievement effects moderated by discipline, motivation by education level. Authors caution that strength of evidence depends on primary study validity. — [Göksu & Bolat 2021](https://gcris.artuklu.edu.tr/entities/publication/bcdc76ff-9439-4f73-afb2-9934efe40eb6)
- **Limitations:** A motivational design overlay, not a content sequence. Primary studies are mostly classroom or e-learning interventions of full lessons, mostly quasi-experimental; it says nothing about how to structure explanation itself. Effects of "attention gimmicks" on transfer are not established.
- **Fit:** Good as a **layer**, especially "Relevance" (why this matters in CS practice) and "Confidence" (signal that it's learnable; success on the first check). Do not give ARCS its own segment.
- **Mapping:** Fold A+R into the hook (10 s: surprising failure case that shows relevance), C into the worked example (success is within reach, steps small), S into the answer-reveal/feedback beat.

### 2.5 4C/ID (Four-Component Instructional Design)
- **Origin [BK]:** van Merrienboer (1997; 2nd ed. with Kirschner 2018). Four components: learning tasks (whole-task, varying), supportive information, procedural information (just-in-time), part-task practice. Scaffolding fades as expertise increases.
- **Evidence (MODERATE):** Costa, Miranda & Melo (2022), *Learning Environments Research*: meta-analysis of 4C/ID programs, pooled **d = 0.79** on performance (knowledge and complex skills), higher-education level a significant moderator. I only saw repository abstracts, not the full paper. — [ISCTE repository](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/publications/the-four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-a-meta-analysis-on-use-and-effect/81383?lang=en)
- **Limitations:** Course-level design model for complex skills; effect confounded with complete redesign and typically compared against conventional curricula, not against other models. Not designed for a single 75-s unit.
- **Fit:** Poor as a script structure; strong as a **cross-video design principle**: sequencing of tasks from simple to complex with fading support, just-in-time procedural info, and part-task practice. For leveled content, the "completion strategy" (worked example → completion problem → full problem) is the best-evidenced scaffolding fade. **[BK]**
- **Mapping:** Within a script: the "Deliver 2" worked example becomes support-fading across levels (L1 fully worked, L2 partially completed with a blank step, L3 problem only, answer revealed after pause).

### 2.6 Elaboration Theory
- **Origin [BK]:** Reigeluth & Stein (1983). Epitome first (simplest, most fundamental version of the whole), then successive elaborations; with synthesizers, summarisers, analogies, cognitive-strategy activators.
- **Evidence:** Not located in this session. Historically small-scale studies with mixed support. **[BK]**, treat as WEAK.
- **Fit:** Good as a **cross-video sequencing** rule (a course map of knowledge units from epitome to detail). Within a video: "epitome first" is the same as the problem-first/overview-first advice; mapping equals "one-sentence overview, then one example, then detail depending on level".
- **Level mapping:** L1 stops at epitome + one example; L3 elaborates edge cases or complexity.

### 2.7 Bloom-aligned / constructive alignment designs
- **Origin [BK]:** Bloom et al. (1956), Anderson & Krathwohl (2001) revision; Biggs (1996) constructive alignment: outcome verbs, teaching activities and assessment aligned.
- **Evidence:** I did not find controlled evidence for alignment per se in this session. The 2025 Heliyon microlearning framework categorises outcomes by Bloom but is a systematic-review-derived framework, not a tested one. — [Heliyon via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2b4da6d99a2a49608c3fde49b9b3174d). Alignment is widely taught, weakly tested (WEAK).
- **Fit:** Not a sequence, but a **constraint**: the objective, the practice prompt and the end-of-video check must share the same cognitive verb (e.g., "trace", "predict", not "know"). This is cheap to implement and valuable for both leveling (Bloom level by learner level) and automated quality checks.
- **Mapping:** Objective verb sets the hook question and the retrieval prompt; L1 = remember/understand/apply; L3 = analyse/evaluate/create.

### 2.8 5E and Predict-Observe-Explain (POE)
- **Origin [BK]:** 5E (Bybee, BSCS, 1989): Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate; POE: White & Gunstone (1992). Both are inquiry/conceptual-change cycles for science teaching.
- **Evidence (MODERATE by volume, low by quality):** Learning-cycle meta-analyses report very large effects: 123 studies, Hedges' g = 1.121 for science achievement; 5E in acid-base chemistry g = 1.845; 5E in physics (2024, 2013-2023 studies) 1.058; level moderator found at university in one analysis but not in another. These are mostly from lower-impact journals with small-sample studies; likely inflated by publication bias. No standalone POE meta-analysis was found. — [Turkish learning-cycle meta-analyses (search aggregate)](https://openaccess.artvin.edu.tr/items/92f0e7cc-da9a-4f2e-860d-57801b98b1d2); [5E physics meta-analysis](https://journal.uinjkt.ac.id/index.php/edusains/article/view/36339)
- **Limitations:** Built for classrooms with hands-on exploration; "Explore" and "Observe" need real or simulated phenomena. POE depends on learner actually committing to a prediction.
- **Fit:** POE is the **best match for animated CS videos** (the animation can show the "observation": run the code, trace the algorithm). It front-loads a retrieval-like generative act (prediction) and exploits the pretesting/prequestion effect. 5E is too long.
- **Mapping:** Predict (10 s: "what will this code print? pause") → Observe (15 s: animation runs, result shown) → Explain (25 s: the concept resolves the discrepancy) → Elaborate (15 s: change one parameter or new case) → (optional) Evaluate (10 s: one-question check).
- **Related evidence on the mechanism:** Prequestions produce a moderate specific effect on questioned content (g = 0.54, k = 97, preregistered meta-analysis, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*), and prequestions inserted before video segments improved retention of questioned and non-questioned material (Carpenter & Toftness). — [Springer s00426-024-02042-8 / search aggregate](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-022-01392-1) (note: search snippet attributed the g = 0.54 meta-analysis to a 2024 PB&R paper; the exact URL for that paper was not confirmed)

### 2.9 BOPPPS
- **Origin [BK]:** Instructional Skills Workshop (UBC, 1970s; Pat Hurst), widely adopted in China. Bridge-in, Objectives, Pre-assessment, Participatory learning, Post-assessment, Summary.
- **Evidence (MODERATE, mostly Chinese health education vs lecture):** Ma et al. (2022, *Frontiers in Medicine*): 41 studies (34 RCTs), 5,042 medical students; BOPPPS better than lecture on knowledge, skill and comprehensive ability scores and satisfaction. Li et al. (2024, *Frontiers in Medicine*): nursing, 13 RCTs, 2,991 students; higher theory, practice and self-learning scores; evidence graded low-to-very-low for some outcomes and moderate for self-learning. A BMC Med Educ 2025 review and a 2026 AI-enhanced BOPPPS RCT meta-analysis also exist, results not retrieved. — [Li 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2024.1319711/full); [Ma 2022](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.975229/text); [BMC 2025](https://bmcmededuc.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12909-025-07274-9)
- **Limitations:** Compared with lecture, so it conflates structure with added activity. Unblinded, small studies; regional concentration.
- **Fit:** Very close to CLT-bLM's skeleton (Bridge-in ≈ Prepare, Objectives ≈ Initiate, Participatory + Post-assessment ≈ Deliver/End) but **adds pre-assessment and post-assessment inside the unit**. Because BOPPPS explicitly includes a post-test and participatory step, it is a natural, minimal upgrade of the existing structure.
- **Mapping:** Bridge-in hook (8 s) → Objective (5 s) → Pre-assessment (one question with pause, 8 s, which doubles as prequestion) → Participatory (core idea + example with a prompt, 35 s) → Post-assessment (1 question + reveal, 12 s) → Summary (7 s).

### 2.10 ADDIE-adjacent "Context-Content-Practice" style
- **Origin:** I could not identify a canonical source or evidence base for "Context-Content-Practice" as a named framework; it appears to be a practitioner pattern (frame the situation, present content, then practice) sitting inside ADDIE-type production workflows. No empirical evaluation found. **Treat as WEAK/unverified.**
- **Mapping:** Context (hook + objective, 12 s) → Content (core + example, 40 s) → Practice (retrieval prompt + feedback, 18 s) → (optional) wrap-up. It differs from CLT-bLM mostly by making practice a named, required phase.

### 2.11 Dick & Carey instructional events
- **Origin [BK]:** Dick, Carey & Carey, *The Systematic Design of Instruction* (1st ed. 1978). The "instructional strategy" component prescribes: pre-instructional activities (motivation, objectives, entry skills), content presentation, learner participation (practice and feedback), assessment, follow-through (memory aids, transfer). A close relative of Gagne.
- **Evidence:** Not located; the model is a systems design process, so its evidence base is that of the underlying practices. WEAK as a distinct framework.
- **Mapping:** Pre-instructional (hook + objective + entry-skill cue, 15 s) → presentation (25 s) → participation (pause prompt + feedback, 20 s) → assessment (single item, 10 s) → follow-through (transfer/next unit pointer, 5 s).

### 2.12 Microlearning-specific variants and adjacent short-form research
- **Microlearning evidence base (MODERATE-WEAK):** Senadheera et al. (2024), higher-ed meta-analysis of five studies (n = 344 vs 310) found higher academic performance for microlearning vs macro-learning, pooled difference 12.6 (95% CI 1.2-23.9, p = 0.03). Geriatric-medicine meta-analysis (*Age and Ageing*, 2026): delirium knowledge SMD 0.80, recognition SMD 0.91; 90% of 40 outcomes positive, only 30% statistically significant. Monib et al. (*Heliyon*, 2024): systematic review categorised by Bloom with a proposed framework; no pooled effect. — [Senadheera et al. via JMTR](https://journals.kln.ac.lk/jmtr/media/attachments/2024/08/28/jmtr_24_22.pdf); [Monib et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11774797/)
- **Named microlearning models seen:** MILE (TD course), "Essential Action Framework" (Brandon Hall), Bite-Sized Teaching model (medical education), Heliyon 2025 CLT/Andragogy/Bloom framework. None has controlled evidence comparing event structures. — [TD](https://courses.td.org/diweb/catalog/item/id/946338); [Brandon Hall](https://institute.brandonhall.com/?p=61421)
- **Active learning in video:** A meta-analysis of active learning strategies in video learning (apparently Zhang, Li, Pi & Yang, 2019-2025 studies, embedded questions the most common strategy, 46%) exists, but I could not confirm its effect size. — [Scribd copy, provenance unverified](https://www.scribd.com/document/924187808/Active-Learning-Strategies-in-Video-Learning-a-Meta-Analysis)

### Inferences
- Event-sequence frameworks with a good meta-analytic result (Gagne, BOPPPS) are mostly validated against lecture, a weak comparator, and with heavy design-quality inflation (Gagne RCT-only SMD 0.43 vs cohort 2.20). Neither has been compared head-to-head with a simpler alternative such as "tell + one retrieval question".
- The frameworks that matter for a 75 s video are those whose active ingredient is something learners do: predict, answer, self-explain.

### Gaps
- No evidence located for Component Display Theory, Elaboration Theory, Dick & Carey, or "Context-Content-Practice" in this session; their profiles rely on [BK].
- No source for Gagne 1965/1985, Merrill 2002, Keller 1987, van Merrienboer 1997, Reigeluth 1983, Bybee 1989 original bibliographic details was fetched; citations of origin are [BK].
- No study found that compares micro-video event structures head-to-head (e.g., problem-first vs concept-first in sub-2-minute videos).

## 3. Does following Gagne/Merrill-style event sequences improve outcomes versus control designs?

### Takeaway
Weakly-to-moderately. Meta-analyses show positive effects versus lecture (Gagne SMD ~0.4 in RCTs and ~1.55 overall; BOPPPS positive on knowledge in 13-34 RCTs), but comparators are weak, quality is low, heterogeneity is extreme and no study isolates the sequence from added activities. For Merrill, the evidence is largely correlational with one MOOC experiment; no meta-analysis exists.

### Cited Findings
- Gagne meta-analysis (Li et al. 2025): overall knowledge SMD 1.55 (I² = 95%); RCT-only 0.43 (0.17-0.68, I² = 0%), cohort-only 2.20; practice SMD 1.83; compliance OR 4.92; satisfaction OR 7.86; low study quality, small samples, unblinded. — [Frontiers in Medicine 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1522830/full)
- BOPPPS meta-analyses (13 RCTs/2,991 nursing students; 41 studies/34 RCTs/5,042 medical students) report better knowledge/skill scores than lecture; evidence graded low to very low for some outcomes, moderate for self-learning. — [Li 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2024.1319711/full); [Ma 2022](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.975229/text)
- Merrill: Frick et al. 2010 association (~4x odds of high mastery); authors note few empirical tests; MOOC experiment (Badali et al. 2022, n = 335) favoured Merrill design; no pooled effect size found. — [Frick](https://tedfrick.sitehost.iu.edu/AECTresearchAwardFrick.pdf); [Badali 2022](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/669f714a00eb85b7d72d0046)
- ARCS: ES 0.74 on achievement, 0.43 on motivation (38 controlled studies). — [Göksu & Bolat 2021](https://gcris.artuklu.edu.tr/entities/publication/bcdc76ff-9439-4f73-afb2-9934efe40eb6)
- 4C/ID: pooled d = 0.79 (abstract-level only). — [Costa et al. 2022](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/publications/the-four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-a-meta-analysis-on-use-and-effect/81383?lang=en)
- The Merrill paper reporting non-universality of first principles (2019, *ETR&D*). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11423-019-09660-9)

### Inferences
- Realistic expectation for a structured sequence versus unstructured lecture: small-to-medium (d ≈ 0.3-0.5, matching the Gagne RCT subgroup and the typical 0.4 pattern), not the ~1-2 values from low-quality designs.
- Because no control compares sequences with one another, moving from CLT-bLM to Gagne/Merrill is not supported by direct evidence; any claim of "outperform" must come from the effect of the underlying mechanisms (retrieval, prediction, worked examples), which have stronger evidence (Section 4).

### Gaps
- No head-to-head comparisons between event models; no studies on videos under ~5 minutes.

## 4. Which frameworks put retrieval/application inside the learning unit, and what does it mean for a 60-90 s video?

### Takeaway
Gagne (events 6-8), Merrill (application, integration), BOPPPS (pre/post-assessment), Component Display Theory (practice), 4C/ID (part-task practice), POE (predict) and CLT-bLM's End phase (quizzes, self-explanation) all place learner activity inside the unit. The project's current script does not (End is a recap). Retrieval practice has a robust independent evidence base, so adding a pause-and-answer beat is the most evidence-backed single change.

### Cited Findings
- Practice testing beats restudy and other conditions: Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan (2017), *Review of Educational Research* 87(3), 659-701, 118 articles, 272 effect sizes, ~15,000 participants; the commonly quoted g = 0.61 (secondary sources say possibly the fixed-effects figure; random-effects reported as 0.70); classroom setting g ~0.67; multiple-choice stronger than short-answer; feedback effect not significant. — [WSU record](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Rethinking-the-Use-of-Tests-A/99900601154801842); [secondary summaries](https://www.tieonline.com/article/2232/the-benefits-of-practice-testing)
- Retrieval practice in mathematics is inconclusive: Murray, Horner & Göbel (2025), 7 studies, 32 effect sizes. — [search result aggregate](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/229807) (provenance of the specific URL not confirmed)
- Prequestions: moderate specific effect g = 0.54 (k = 97), preregistered meta-analysis; Carpenter & Toftness show prequestions before video segments improve retention. — [Springer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-022-01392-1); [Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-024-02042-8)
- Embedded questions in video: a dissertation found short-answer embedded questions beat restudying the video on immediate and delayed tests; a Twente thesis found no difference between retention and compensatory embedded questions. Both low-evidence. — [UMSL dissertation](https://irl.umsl.edu/dissertation/1282); [Twente thesis](https://essay.utwente.nl/essays/79607)
- CLT-bLM's End phase explicitly includes self-explanation prompts and short quizzes. — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)

### Inferences
- In 60-90 s, the "pause" is the design problem: a narrated video can insert 3-5 s of silence plus on-screen prompt. A single prediction/prequestion (early) and a single retrieval question (late) are feasible; more than two would eat 20-25% of the runtime.
- A recap (Summary) is a re-presentation, not retrieval. Replacing the recap with "retrieve, then reveal the summary" retains the same duration and adds retrieval.
- Effects for retrieval transfer less reliably to complex or mathematical material (cf. inconclusive maths meta-analysis), so for CS, use application-level questions (trace/predict), not recall of definitions.

### Gaps
- Could not verify primary effect sizes for embedded questions in video; Zhang et al. meta-analysis unreadable.
- Whether delayed retention gain from a single 3-5 s pause exists for 60-90 s video is not tested in the literature found.

## 5. Which frameworks are best suited to adaptive/leveled instruction (novice vs advanced)?

### Takeaway
4C/ID (support fading), Component Display Theory (performance levels), Elaboration Theory (epitome to detail), and Bloom-aligned design (verb levels) are explicitly leveled. Productive-failure evidence indicates problem-first benefits depend on prior knowledge and task type, which supports varying the order by level. Merrill and Gagne are level-agnostic.

### Cited Findings
- Productive failure (problem solving then instruction) meta-analysis (Sinha & Kapur 2021, *Review of Educational Research*, DOI 10.3102/00346543211019105): 53 studies, 166 comparisons; headline bias-adjusted g reported as 0.87 in one summary, while an EdArXiv version reports g = 0.55 vs scaffolded problem-solving and 0.64 vs instruction-first, and a non-significant 0.22 for one problem-solving-first vs instruction-first comparison; I could not confirm which numbers are in the published headline. Sinha notes that for procedural tasks (e.g., computing a standard deviation) instruction-first works as well. — [Sage DOI mirror via search](https://https-sage-cnpereading-com-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105); [THE Campus](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/using-productive-failure-activate-deeper-learning) (the first link is a mirror URL on a university proxy domain; use the DOI)
- 4C/ID meta-analysis: higher-education is a significant moderator. — [Costa et al. 2022](https://ciencia.iscte-iul.pt/publications/the-four-component-instructional-design-model-4cid-a-meta-analysis-on-use-and-effect/81383?lang=en)
- ARCS: achievement effects vary by discipline; motivation effects by education level. — [Göksu & Bolat 2021](https://gcris.artuklu.edu.tr/entities/publication/bcdc76ff-9439-4f73-afb2-9934efe40eb6)
- Adaptive microlearning for cognitive load in in-service personnel (Sci Rep 2024). — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-77122-1) (abstract-level only)
- [BK] Expertise reversal effect (Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller, 2003): worked examples and heavy guidance help novices but can hurt experts, so level-specific scripts should differ in guidance and not just vocabulary. Not retrieved in this session; verify before citing.

### Inferences
- Proposed leveling rules (not directly tested for video):
  - Level 1: concept-first or worked-example-first, with a short, low-stakes prediction on a familiar case, fully worked example, and a recognition-level retrieval question.
  - Level 2: partial worked example (completion problem) and application-level question.
  - Level 3: problem or prediction first (productive-failure style), brief concept reveal, no full worked example, transfer question to a new context.
- Elaboration Theory suggests keeping the same epitome across levels and varying the elaboration, which simplifies content production.
- Bloom-aligned verbs give a deterministic hook for level adaptation.

### Gaps
- No direct experiments on leveled micro-video scripts.
- Could not confirm the published headline effect for productive failure.

## 6. Which frameworks would plausibly beat Prepare/Initiate/Deliver/End, and why (problem-first vs concept-first)?

### Takeaway
No source tests this directly. The best-supported modifications are: (1) add a prediction/prequestion early (POE, BOPPPS pre-assessment, Merrill problem-centred), (2) replace the final recap with a retrieval question plus reveal (Gagne 6-8, BOPPPS post-assessment, CLT-bLM End), and (3) vary the worked-example support and the problem-first/concept-first order by level (4C/ID, productive failure, expertise reversal). Keep CLT/CTML production rules as constraints.

### Cited Findings
- All findings listed in Sections 2-5 apply; key numbers: practice testing g ≈ 0.61-0.70; prequestions g = 0.54; productive failure g between 0.22 and 0.87 depending on comparison and estimate; Gagne RCT subgroup SMD 0.43; ARCS 0.74/0.43; BOPPPS positive vs lecture. (Sources as above.)
- CLT-bLM's own authors include prior-knowledge activation and quizzes, so the restructured scripts remain consistent with the model's principles. — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)

### Inferences (candidate script structures, ranked by my judgment, not by evidence)

**Candidate A: Predict-Reveal-Explain-Apply-Recall (POE + Merrill + BOPPPS hybrid; best expected gain, leveled)** — total ~75 s
1. Hook as problem/prediction (8-10 s): a concrete CS scenario ("What does this loop print?"). Folds in ARCS Attention and Relevance and the objective as a clause.
2. Predict pause (3-4 s, on-screen prompt) — prequestion/pre-assessment; level 3 may be a harder problem.
3. Observe/Reveal (12-15 s): animation shows the result; discrepancy appears.
4. Explain core idea (15-20 s): concept stated after the problem, tied to the observation (segmenting, signaling, coherence).
5. Worked example (15-20 s): Level 1 full, Level 2 one step blank, Level 3 skipped or replaced by variation.
6. Retrieve/Apply (8-10 s incl. pause): one new question at the stated Bloom verb, then answer.
7. Integrate (5 s): one-line takeaway and where it reappears (replaces recap).

**Candidate B: Minimal upgrade of the current script (lowest risk)** — keep five segments but (a) move objective into Prepare, (b) turn Initiate into a prequestion with a pause, (c) turn End into "retrieve then reveal summary" and (d) keep Deliver 1 + Deliver 2 as is. This is essentially BOPPPS and CLT-bLM's own End phase made explicit.

**Candidate C: Concept-first with CDT template for procedural units** — for step-based units (e.g., "how to compute X"), instruction-first can match problem-first (Sinha's comment); use Generality → Example → Non-example → Practice → Feedback.

- Problem-first vs concept-first rule of thumb: use problem-first/predict for conceptual and principle units where learners have some prior knowledge (levels 2-3); use worked-example-first for novices and procedural units (level 1).
- Why plausible: adds two forms of retrieval (pre- and post-), moves from telling to a generate-then-explain cycle, and supports leveled fading, all with evidence-backed mechanisms while respecting the 60-90 s limit. Why uncertain: 3-5 s pauses may be skipped by viewers; prediction effects in <2 min videos are untested; the added beats compress core content.

### Gaps
- No empirical test of any of these structures in 60-90 s videos; recommendation is to run an A/B evaluation (delayed post-test at 1 week; transfer item; per learner level) comparing current script, Candidate A, and Candidate B.
- Evidence strength ranking above is a judgment, not from a source.
