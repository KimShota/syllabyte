# Microlearning instructional frameworks compared

Scope note: research was done with web search and page fetch only. Several key sources (PMC, ResearchGate, Springer ETR&D, Taylor & Francis) were blocked or returned only snippets, so many items below are marked "secondhand" or "unverified". Where a figure came only from a search-engine summary and not from the paper itself, it is flagged. Nothing here was filled in from memory unless explicitly labelled.

## 1. What is CLT-bLM, who proposed it, and what is its evidence base?

### Takeaway
CLT-bLM is the **Cognitive Load Theory-based Lecture Model** (Hadie and colleagues, Universiti Malaysia Sarawak / Universiti Sains Malaysia). It is a guideline for **ordinary (roughly one-hour) face-to-face or online lectures** in health-professions education, not a microlearning model. It has four phases (Preparation, Initiation, Delivery, End) with 27 strategies. The project proposal's attribution to "a Microlearning paper's CLT-bLM model" appears to be a mislabel. I found no source that applies CLT-bLM to 60-90 second videos, and no "Deliver x2" structure is part of the original model (that looks like a project adaptation). Its evidence base is small, single-group-of-authors, health-professions only, and partly reported with internal inconsistencies.

### Cited Findings
- The search "CLT-bLM" resolves to "Cognitive Load Theory-based Lecture Model (CLT-bLM): An Evidence-based Strategy For Effective Lecturing" (Hadie, ResearchGate record dated 2016); a search summary describes it as a step-by-step approach to preparing and delivering lectures — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309633360_Cognitive_Load_Theory-based_Lecture_Model_CLT-bLM_An_Evidence-based_Strategy_For_Effective_Lecturing) (page itself returned 403; only the title and snippet were seen, so authorship/venue of this particular item is not confirmed by me).
- The Frontiers in Medicine 2021 paper (Hadie et al.) says the model is built on cognitive load theory (CLT) and the cognitive theory of multimedia learning (CTML), with **four phases and 27 strategies**: Preparation, Initiation, Delivery, End — [Frontiers in Medicine 2021, 10.3389/fmed.2021.739238](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full)
  - Preparation: taxonomy-based learning outcomes, chunking content, managing visual presentation (reduce intrinsic and extraneous load).
  - Initiation: short pre-lecture activity to activate prior knowledge, focus attention, state the lesson purpose (increase germane load).
  - Delivery: revisit prior knowledge, pacing, appropriate voice, avoid distracting behaviours, intra-lecture activities, short break after 10-15 minutes, dual-mode presentation, examples and analogies (reduce extraneous load).
  - End: summaries, short quizzes, self-explanation questions, post-class consultation (support processing and self-assessment).
  - Source for the phase table: [PMC8520998 version of the same paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8520998/).
- The Frontiers paper identifies the model's origin as Hadie et al. 2018 (a metacognitive CLT-based lecture model, *Innovations in Education and Teaching International* 55(4)), Hadie et al. 2018 (*Journal of Taibah University Medical Sciences*, anatomy lectures), and Hadie's 2017 doctoral thesis — [Frontiers in Medicine 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full). Note: bibliographic records disagree on year for the ITEI paper (repository lists 2016, CV lists 55(4) 398-407, i.e. 2018) — [UNIMAS repository](https://ir.unimas.my/id/eprint/14472/); [search result summary of CV](https://eduimed.usm.my/cvEBM/CV%20Siti%20Nurma%20Hanim%202021.pdf).
- Original trial (ITEI): described in repository text as a randomised controlled trial comparing groups on cognitive load, self-perceived learning, knowledge acquisition and knowledge retention; Frontiers 2021 summarises it as multi-centre RCT in medical anatomy lectures that improved knowledge acquisition and retention, self-perceived learning and cognitive engagement and reduced cognitive load — [UNIMAS repository](https://ir.unimas.my/id/eprint/14472/); [PMC8520998](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8520998/). I could not retrieve the abstract with numbers, so sample size and effect sizes for this RCT are unverified. A search summary mentioned a 99-student trial from two Malaysian universities and "effect sizes 0.404 to 1.696" for lower mental load; neither figure could be traced to a primary page and both should be treated as unverified.
- Second RCT (Journal of Taibah University Medical Sciences 2018): 197 participants from three institutions; control = "freestyle" heart-anatomy lecture, intervention = CLT-bLM lecture on a similar topic by the same lecturer three weeks later; outcome = engagement and motivation questionnaire immediately after; authors conclude the model stimulated cognitive engagement. Not confirmed from the primary page (DOAJ returned 403) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/2b8c146510154edc95b2f2022b693e2e), via search summaries only. Note the design is a fixed-order comparison (control first, intervention three weeks later) per that summary.
- Third study (Frontiers in Medicine 2021, COVID online lectures): pre-post quasi-experimental, n = 215 (106 medicine, 54 dentistry, 55 nutrition), Universiti Sains Malaysia. Intrinsic load fell 2.4 points (t = -11.2, p < 0.001), extraneous load fell 1.1 (t = -6.7, p < 0.001), engagement +0.54, comprehension gains larger for CLT lecture (about 54 vs 35 points, p < 0.001). **No effect sizes reported**; no control group; the two lectures differed in topic, mode (face-to-face vs online), lecturer training and timing; the comprehension test had only five items; motivation result is internally inconsistent (text says improved, Table 1 shows 5.8 to 5.6); regression R2 about 0.07-0.08 — [Frontiers in Medicine 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.739238/full) (fetched summary; last 35k characters unread).
- A 2026 review in *Anatomical Sciences Education* (Rizvi, Hadie and colleagues) discusses CLT-based strategies in anatomy teaching; not a trial — [UNIMAS scholarhub](https://scholarhub.unimas.my/items/4dd50f0b-921c-400b-86d2-58ca5bdf073f) (search summary only).
- Ruled out as the source: Khong & Kabilan's "A theoretical model of micro-learning for second language instruction" (*Computer Assisted Language Learning*, 2020/2022 depending on record) is a CLT + CTML + self-determination theory model for L2 learning; no Prepare/Initiate/Deliver/End phases were found — [UniKL repository](https://ir.unikl.edu.my/jspui/handle/123456789/25271) (search summary; full text not seen). The Sachdeva-style microlearning/CLT papers and the Indian/Pakistani CLT-microlearning studies (e.g. EJ1423546, a post-test control group study in higher education) mention CLT only generally and do not contain CLT-bLM; I extracted the full text of EJ1423546 and found no "CLT-bLM", "Prepare", or "Initiate" — [ERIC EJ1423546](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1423546.pdf).

### Inferences
- The project's four-stage structure maps onto CLT-bLM's lecture phases, but CLT-bLM's own justification (breaks every 10-15 min, intra-lecture activities, lecturer voice and behaviour, consultation) is lecture-specific and does not transfer directly to a 60-90 s narrated video. The "Deliver x2" element is not in the source model.
- The CLT-bLM evidence supports "CLT-informed lecturing helps" at the level of self-reported load and engagement and some comprehension gain, in health-professions students; it does not isolate which of the 27 strategies matter, and the confounds are substantial. It should be cited as "lecture design guideline grounded in CLT/CTML" rather than as a validated microlearning model.
- Practical recommendation for the proposal text: rename it "Cognitive Load Theory-based Lecture Model (CLT-bLM; Hadie et al., 2018)" and state that it is being adapted to micro-videos.

### Gaps
- Primary abstracts/effect sizes for the 2018 ITEI RCT and 2018 Taibah RCT were not retrievable (403/timeouts). Verify the "d = 0.404-1.696" figure and sample sizes before citing.
- No source found that tests CLT-bLM on videos shorter than a lecture, or in CS/STEM.
- Exact bibliographic year of the ITEI paper (2016 online-first vs 2018 issue) unresolved.

## 2. Catalogue of other microlearning frameworks, design principles and models

### Takeaway
Microlearning has many descriptive/definitional frameworks (Hug's dimensions, Jomah, Monib, Keshmiri) but few are tested as a whole. The most consistently recurring principles across reviews are: single learning objective per unit, short duration, interactivity/assessment, immediate feedback, multimedia (CLT/CTML) design, and spaced or flexible delivery. The frameworks that explicitly build in retrieval and spacing are those from the health-professions "spaced education" tradition, which is where the quantified evidence is.

### Cited Findings
- **Hug (2005) dimensions.** Micro-learning described along dimensions of time, content, curriculum, form, process, mediality and learning type; sources disagree on whether it is six or seven dimensions. Hug characterises microlearning as brief lessons focused on a single objective; he states there is no single precise definition — [Hug introductory note](https://researchgate.net/profile/Theo-Hug/publication/237397162_Microlearning_A_New_Pedagogical_Challenge_Introductory_Note/links/54009baf0cf23d9765a3f648/Microlearning-A-New-Pedagogical-Challenge-Introductory-Note.pdf) (snippet only); dimension counts from [Alias & Razak 2023 PDF](https://e-journal.uum.edu.my/index.php/mjli/article/download/19329/4019/66815) and other snippets. Critique: Hug's dimensions lack a pedagogical component (secondhand, search snippet).
- **Jomah et al. (2016).** Framework listing curriculum, form, process, mediality and learning type; presented as a "modernised education system" (BRAIN journal) — [BRAIN PDF](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/brain/brain-582.pdf) (snippet only). Descriptive; no effectiveness data seen.
- **Giurgiu (2017).** Only seen as a secondhand citation for the idea that microlearning is a stand-alone manageable content unit, not merely truncated information. No details found. **Shail (2019): nothing found.**
- **Taylor & Hung (2022), "The effects of microlearning: a scoping review," *Educational Technology Research and Development* 70(2), 363-395.** Title/venue confirmed via other papers' reference lists; the paper itself and abstract were not retrievable. A 2026 meta-analysis (secondhand) credits it with a positive effect on knowledge and confidence in health professions — [search result summaries; e.g. Monib et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11774797/). Note: it is a scoping review, not a systematic review.
- **De Gagne et al. (2019), "Microlearning in Health Professions Education: Scoping Review," *JMIR Medical Education* 5(2):e13997.** JBI scoping methodology, multiple databases (PubMed, CINAHL, ERIC, EMBASE, PsycINFO, etc.). Reported benefits: knowledge and confidence performing procedures, retention, studying, collaborative learning; downsides: pedagogical discomfort, technology inequality, privacy; technologies were podcast, SMS, microblogging, social networks (not narrated animated video); **no studies at the highest outcome level** (patient/practice outcomes) — [PMC6683654](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6683654/) (via search summary).
- **Leong, Sung, Au & Blanchard (2021), "A review of the trend of microlearning," *Journal of Work-Applied Management* 13(1), 88-102.** Citation confirmed from a reference list; findings not retrieved. I did not find a "Yin Ho et al. 2021" review. — [reference list snippet via search](https://e-journal.uum.edu.my/index.php/mjli/article/view/19329)
- **Alias & Razak (2023), PRISMA systematic review of microlearning pedagogy in higher education, 2014-2023,** *Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction*: themes = design of microlearning content and instructional flow — [MJLI](https://e-journal.uum.edu.my/index.php/mjli/article/view/19329) (search summary).
- **Keshmiri, Heidari & Mehrparvar (2025), scoping review,** *Journal of Medical Education and Development* 19(4):986-1000. Two-part framework: **content design** (interactive content; chunks each with a single learning objective; short time frames) and **flow design** (social-supportive structure, varied media, immediate feedback); recommends CLT and multimedia-learning strategies — [JMED abstract](https://jmed.ssu.ac.ir/article-1-1515-en.html); [KNE publishing record](https://publish.kne-publishing.com/index.php/JMED/article/view/18182).
- **Monib, Qazi & Apong (2024/2025), "Microlearning beyond boundaries," *Heliyon* 11(2):e41413.** 47 studies appraised, 40 analysed (2020-2024). Framework with three components: learner/situational/subject differences (from Andragogy in Practice), **guiding principles** (specific objective, bite-sized content, appropriate timeframe, interactive and engaging content, personalisation, suitable medium/mode), and outcomes (cognitive, behavioural, affective by Bloom). Concludes microlearning has a positive impact but notes results are "scattered and mixed", no agreed definition, content length 1-15 min with no agreed ideal, studies concentrated in medicine/healthcare, framework examples are illustrative not tested — [PMC11774797 (via Europe PMC XML)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11774797/fullTextXML).
- **Spanish-language review (2018-2021):** prevailing content structure = introduction + microcontent + questions, with heavy emphasis on educational video — [search summary of review](https://gredos.usal.es/handle/10366/156135) (snippet only, not verified).
- **MICROS framework** (six stages: Map, Identify, Create, Respond, Operate, Summarise), Indonesian study; seen only in a search summary, no evidence reviewed — [IJLTER-type source not fetched; search result only].
- **Spaced-education / retrieval-based microlearning (Kerfoot-type)**: see Section 3; these are the only microlearning-adjacent designs with pooled quantitative evidence and explicit embedded retrieval plus spacing — [Spaced Digital Education meta-analysis, JMIR 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11502984/).
- **Chinese micro-lecture ("weike") models, Duolingo-style micro-lessons, Kapp/Dreyfus-style frameworks, Learning Nuggets, Bruck/Motiwalla/Foerster:** searches did not return usable primary or review sources (see Gaps). One search of weike meta-analyses found none in English-language indexes.

### Inferences
- Reviews converge on a de facto framework that closely matches the project's script: one objective, chunked content, multimedia-principle design, interactive element/quiz, feedback. This convergence is from reviews of mostly low-rigor studies, so it is consensus, not tested causal evidence.
- Hug and Jomah are taxonomies of what microlearning is, not instructional-design procedures with evidence; they are unsuitable as an alternative to CLT-bLM for script structure.
- The more evidence-backed "alternatives" for within-unit structure are not named microlearning models at all but the CTML/CLT principles (segmenting, signalling, coherence) and retrieval practice (see Section 4).

### Gaps
- No usable source for Shail 2019, Giurgiu 2017 details, Bruck/Motiwalla/Foerster, Learning Nuggets, Duolingo effectiveness, Kapp frameworks, or Chinese weike design models. Leong 2021 and Taylor & Hung 2022 content not read. No "Yin Ho et al. 2021" found.

## 3. Controlled studies and meta-analyses: effect sizes and quality caveats

### Takeaway
Recent meta-analyses report moderate-to-large positive pooled effects for microlearning (SMD roughly 0.7-0.9 in two 2025 items, mean difference 12.6 marks in another), but with very high heterogeneity (I2 66-82%), small numbers of studies in the best-controlled analysis, possible publication bias, and comparators that often differ in more than length. Where spacing and testing are isolated (spaced digital education), effects are smaller (SMD 0.32 for knowledge). I found no RCT of short video microlearning for university CS/STEM students.

### Cited Findings
- **Senadheera et al. (2024), "Impact of microlearning on academic performance of students in higher education: systematic review and meta-analysis," *Journal of Multidisciplinary and Translational Research* 9(1):10-25.** 602 records, 12 studies in review, 5 in meta-analysis (RevMan 5.4); microlearning n = 344 vs macro-learning n = 310; pooled mean difference on theoretical post-tests 12.6 marks (95% CI 1.2 to 23.9), p = 0.03; macro-learning = any comparator session longer than 15 minutes; mechanisms offered: reduced cognitive load, flexibility, self-directed learning, timely feedback — [JMTR PDF](https://journals.kln.ac.lk/jmtr/media/attachments/2024/08/28/jmtr_24_22.pdf) (search summaries; multiple PDF versions exist). Wide CI, k = 5, no standardised effect size.
- **"Microlearning Effectiveness in Higher Education: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Student Retention and Learning Outcomes" (2025, ResearchGate preprint-type record).** Search summary: pooled SMD 0.74 for learning outcomes, I2 = 71%, trim-and-fill suggested possible publication bias; pooled OR 1.87 for student retention (course retention, not knowledge retention) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394265408_Microlearning_Effectiveness_in_Higher_Education_A_Systematic_Review_and_Meta-Analysis_of_Student_Retention_and_Learning_Outcomes) (page returned 403; peer-review status and authors unverified).
- **"Microlearning in Undergraduate Medical Education: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Impact on Knowledge, Skills, and Retention" (ResearchGate record).** Search summary: random-effects Hedges' g pooled SMD 0.90 (95% CI 0.57-1.22), I2 = 82%; leave-one-out and trim-and-fill left estimate unchanged — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/413845628_Microlearning_in_Undergraduate_Medical_Education_A_Systematic_Review_and_Meta-Analysis_of_Impact_on_Knowledge_Skills_and_Retention) (not fetched; unverified, peer-review status unknown).
- **EFL microlearning meta-analysis (IJLTER):** 10 studies, n = 743, SMD 1.43 vs lectures, I2 = 66% — [IJLTER PDF](https://www.ijlter.org/index.php/ijlter/article/download/10006/pdf) (search summary only; language-learning, not STEM).
- **Wang et al. (2020), self-care microlearning review:** 26 manuscripts, narrative synthesis, no pooled effect; cognitive self-care gains but "largely ineffective" at changing actual behaviour — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10112289/) (search summary).
- **Geriatric/delirium microlearning review (PROSPERO-registered):** knowledge SMD 0.80 (95% CI 0.49-1.10), recognition SMD 0.91 (95% CI 0.10-1.72); of 40 outcomes, 90% positive but only 30% statistically significant — [ANZSGM 2025 abstract](https://anzsgm-2025.p.asnevents.com.au/days/2025-06-12/abstract/120049) (conference abstract; weak).
- **Spaced digital education for health professionals (JMIR 2024; 23 studies, 17 online education, 6 simulation).** Spaced vs massed online education, post-intervention knowledge: SMD 0.32 (95% CI 0.13-0.51), k = 9, I2 = 66%, moderate certainty; clinical behaviour SMD 0.67 (95% CI 0.43-0.91), k = 3, moderate certainty; surgical skills (simulation) SMD 1.15, k = 2, low certainty; 83% of studies unclear/high risk of bias; pooled estimates are post-intervention, not delayed retention — [PMC11502984](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11502984/) (search summary).
- **Video in higher education (Noetel et al. 2021, *Review of Educational Research*):** 105 studies, 7,776 students; replacing existing teaching with video g = 0.28 (small); adding video to existing teaching g = 0.80; possible experimental and publication bias — [AERA summary](https://www.aera.net/tabid/19324/mid/50329/default.aspx); critique of "skill vs knowledge" framing in a 2024 preprint — [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202401.0247). Relevance: the effect of video itself is small when it replaces equivalent instruction; large effects mostly come from added exposure.
- **Segmenting effect meta-analysis (Rey et al. 2019, *Educational Psychology Review* 31:389-419):** 56 investigations, 88 comparisons; small-to-medium effects on retention and transfer; segmentation also reduced cognitive load and increased learning time; larger retention benefit for high prior-knowledge learners — [Springer DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S10648-018-9456-4) (search summary; exact pooled d not retrieved). Mayer & Pilegard (2014) cited a median d = 0.79 in 10 of 10 tests (secondhand, not directly comparable).
- **Practice testing meta-analysis (Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan 2017, *Review of Educational Research* 87(3):659-701):** 118 studies; practice tests beat restudying and other comparison conditions; effects moderated by test format and feedback — [WSU PDF](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/). Pooled g not retrieved.
- **Interpolated retrieval:** Chan, Ahn & Szpunar (2025) report g = 0.69 online and 0.76 lab for interpolated retrieval (secondhand, from search summary; only three online studies) — [PMC11965562](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11965562) (not fetched). Embedded video questions: mixed; some studies find gains, others none, and some students find embedded quizzes distracting or anxiety-provoking — [Springer s10639-023-12303-5](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10639-023-12303-5) (search summary).
- **Video-based microlearning in programming:** a quasi-experimental study with intermediate school students (ALShammari 2024) and a single-group study of 30 diploma students (Umm Al-Qura) reported gains; interactive micro-video (embedded questions/cues) outperformed linear micro-video in an OOP achievement test (design unclear); none are RCTs with university STEM students — [JEELR](https://asianonlinejournals.com/index.php/JEELR/article/view/5399); [other results as found by search](https://sec.journals.ekb.eg/article_377545_57662380f2102f1028288d30e470f0df.pdf). A review-cited 85.4 vs 72.6 programming score could not be verified.
- Microlearning in a Malaysian higher-education post-test-only control group study (lecturer-prepared content, both groups simultaneously; reports t-tests and effect size calculations; p < 0.05; reported satisfaction/engagement favoured the microlearning module) — [ERIC EJ1423546](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1423546.pdf) (extracted text; sample size and effect size not extracted). Small single-site design.

### Inferences
- Quality caveats stack: (a) very few randomized comparisons, (b) comparators rarely hold content, time-on-task and assessment constant, (c) microlearning conditions often add spacing, quizzes, feedback and mobile access, so effects are confounded with spacing/testing, (d) outcomes are mostly short-term knowledge tests, (e) the highest numbers (SMD 0.74-1.43) come from items I could not verify as peer-reviewed.
- Because the best-controlled spacing meta-analysis gives SMD 0.32 for knowledge and video-replacement g is 0.28, a realistic expected effect for a single 60-90 s video unit relative to equivalent conventional instruction is small unless it contains retrieval and spacing. This is an inference, not a measured effect.
- "Are effects real or confounded by spacing/testing?" The reviews I could read do not isolate microlearning length from spacing/testing; the spaced-education and practice-testing literatures show spacing and testing are independently effective, which makes confounding likely.

### Gaps
- No pooled estimate isolating segment length (micro vs equal-content macro) while holding spacing and testing constant.
- Primary-source verification of the ResearchGate meta-analyses (0.74, 0.90) and their risk-of-bias ratings is missing.
- No RCT/meta-analysis on micro-video for university CS/STEM; no data on adaptive-to-level microlearning beyond a 2024 *Scientific Reports* adaptive-microlearning cognitive-load study seen in a search snippet (not read) — [Nature s41598-024-77122-1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-77122-1).
- Taylor & Hung 2022 and Sathiyaseelan 2024 findings not retrieved.

## 4. Structural elements associated with better outcomes

### Takeaway
The best-supported elements are not microlearning-specific: retrieval practice/testing, spacing, and segmenting plus other multimedia principles. Microlearning reviews list single objective, short duration, interactivity, feedback, gamification and personalisation as design principles, but they are mostly reported descriptively and not tested as moderators.

### Cited Findings
- **Single learning objective:** recommended in Keshmiri 2025 (content design) and Monib 2025 (guiding principles); not tested as a moderator — [JMED](https://jmed.ssu.ac.ir/article-1-1515-en.html); [PMC11774797](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11774797/fullTextXML).
- **Embedded assessment / retrieval practice:** practice tests outperform restudy across 118 studies (Adesope et al. 2017) — [WSU PDF](https://education.wsu.edu/documents/2018/01/rethinking-use-tests.pdf/); interpolated retrieval g about 0.69-0.76 (secondhand) — [PMC11965562](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11965562). Within microlearning reviews, embedded quizzes appear in several studies, but the Monib excerpt did not link assessment to outcome gains.
- **Spacing:** spaced vs massed digital education SMD 0.32 (knowledge) and 0.67 (clinical behaviour) — [PMC11502984](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11502984/). Monib et al. suggest smaller units over time may help but do not treat spacing as tested, and one included study noted it was unclear whether spacing or repetition changed results.
- **Segmenting (chunking):** small-to-medium effect on retention and transfer, reduced cognitive load, stronger for high prior-knowledge learners — [Rey et al. 2019](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S10648-018-9456-4).
- **Video:** short video preferred and effective in several studies; one study found engagement highest at 5-8 min depending on year of study — [PMC11774797](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11774797/fullTextXML). Replacing teaching with video g = 0.28 — [AERA](https://www.aera.net/tabid/19324/mid/50329/default.aspx).
- **Interactivity:** interactive micro-video (embedded questions/cues) outperformed linear micro-video in an OOP achievement test (design unclear) — see JEELR/other results in Section 3. Embedded quizzes can distract or cause anxiety for some learners — [Springer s10639-023-12303-5](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10639-023-12303-5).
- **Gamification / narrative / personalisation:** presented as engagement/motivation boosters in the Monib review discussion, not tied to measured outcome differences — [PMC11774797](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11774797/fullTextXML). No evidence found on narrative specifically for microlearning.
- **Duration:** no agreed optimum; reported ranges about 1-15 min (Monib); macro comparator in Senadheera = >15 min. No source found on 60-90 s videos specifically.
- **Prior knowledge / adaptivity:** segmenting benefits larger with high prior knowledge (Rey et al.); a 2024 adaptive microlearning study reported reduced unnecessary cognitive load (search snippet) — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-77122-1).
- **Discipline moderator (smart learning environments meta-analysis, not microlearning):** university-level and engineering/technology effects were smaller (0.306; 0.127 not significant) than humanities (0.771) — [Frontiers in Education 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1829962/full). Included only as a caution that effects on technology-enhanced learning tend to be smaller for STEM/engineering and university samples; it is a different intervention class.

### Inferences
- For the project's script (one concept, 60-90 s, narrated animation), the evidence most clearly supports: segmenting plus CTML principles, a retrieval prompt (question) with feedback, and spaced re-exposure across units. The "End" phase quiz in CLT-bLM aligns with retrieval practice; adding spacing across knowledge units would address the confound reviewers raise.
- Embedded questions in a 60-90 s video may be harder to implement than in a lecture, and the evidence on question-in-video is mixed; a post-video retrieval prompt may be a safer design.

### Gaps
- No moderator meta-analysis of microlearning design features (objective, narrative, gamification, interactivity) with effect sizes.
- No evidence found on narrative/storytelling specifically for microlearning outcomes.
- No evidence on videos of 60-90 s duration, or on adaptive-to-level micro-video in CS.
